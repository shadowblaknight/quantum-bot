Verdict — Conditional

The strategy does not work as specified across all 28 pairs — pooled profit factor 0.969, a losing system. But it is not uniformly dead: the failure is concentrated and the survivors share a structural trait.

CHF and JPY crosses are profitable; USD majors and commodity crosses are not. All 7 CHF crosses cleared break-even (odds by chance: 0.78%). After realistic FTMO spread the CHF/JPY basket holds PF 1.207 across 358 trades — a real but modest edge, far weaker than GS1 (2.69) or SP500 (2.14).

CHF and JPY are the funding and safe-haven currencies. Their crosses mean-revert at structural levels — price taps a zone, gets rejected, returns. USD majors are news-driven and trend through the same levels, so a rejection entry is repeatedly run over. The strategy is fundamentally a mean-reversion system, which is why it only pays where mean reversion lives.

Does it survive spread?

The backtest above is frictionless. CHF crosses carry wide spreads (GBPCHF ~2.8 pips) against ~25-pip stops, so cost matters. Modelled at real FTMO spreads:

The edge survives but thins: PF 1.343 → 1.207 at realistic spread, and 1.087 if fills run 2× wide during news. Expectancy falls from +0.159R to +0.102R per trade. There is little margin for slippage.

05

The problem that decides this

⛔ Max drawdown breaches FTMO before it earns

The CHF/JPY basket's worst drawdown is −12.3R. At your normal 1% risk that is a −12.3% account drawdown — past FTMO's 10% total loss limit. This system would have blown the account inside the backtest window.

To hold drawdown under ~6% you would run ~0.5% risk, which halves an already-modest +0.102R expectancy. At ~127 trades/year pooled that is roughly 6.5R/year at 0.5% ≈ 3.2% annual return — against GS1 and SP500 delivering far more at a fraction of the risk.

07

Four bugs found while building this

Each silently produced plausible-looking but wrong results — worth knowing since the same patterns appear in Pine.

  1. Structure detection was dead. ffill().shift(1) compares the previous bar, not the previous pivot — after a forward-fill those are the same value, so HH/HL was never true. Daily bias fired on 0% of bars.
  2. Fibonacci drawn on the wrong leg 51.9% of the time. A bullish retracement requires the swing low to print before the swing high. Without that check the fib sits on down-legs half the time.
  3. Every doji counted as a pin bar. wick ≥ 2 × max(body, 1e-12) is trivially true when the body is ~0 — 18.5% of all bars registered as "rejection confirmations."
  4. Stops sat inside the noise. Measured MAE was 3.93 ATR while stops were ~1 ATR: half of all trades were stopped out before the edge could appear.

The diagnostic that exposed this: a random-entry control beat the strategy (PF 1.14 vs 0.84). An event study then showed the edge is delayed — price moves against entry ~0.23 ATR for 12 bars, then turns positive (+0.31 ATR, 54.4% up by bar 48).

08

Recommendation

  1. Don't build the 28-pair Pine. Pooled PF 0.969 means encoding a losing system.
  2. Don't deploy the CHF/JPY basket to FTMO either — not because the edge is fake, but because −12.3R drawdown breaches the 10% limit. It fails risk, not logic.
  3. If you want to pursue it, scope to the 7 CHF crosses only, forward-test on the $10k personal account where drawdown rules don't apply, and require a full year before considering prop capital.
  4. Higher-value alternative: your ORB architecture (GS1 2.69, SP500 2.14) already outperforms this by 2×. Porting the proven Frankfurt/NY ORB engine to a currency pair is far more likely to pay than rescuing this one.
